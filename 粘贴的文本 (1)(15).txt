Abstract 

Understanding how boundary-layer separation responds to uncertain adverse pressure-gradient conditions remains an important issue in aerodynamics. 

This paper investigates the effect of colored noise on the separation in Falkner-Skan boundary-layer model. The pressure-gradient parameter β is modeled as a stochastic process to represent uncertainty in the effective local pressure-gradient environment. Rather than preserving the classical Falkner–Skan similarity structure in a strict mathematical sense, the present study uses the Falkner–Skan model as a deterministic baseline for a stochastic parametric extension. Correlated stochastic samples are generated through an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, and the associated boundary-value problems are solved in MATLAB. A wall-shear-based indicator is used to quantify separation tendency across stochastic realizations. Monte Carlo simulations are further employed to estimate the probability distribution, mean shift, and confidence intervals of the response under stochastic forcing, while also examining the effects of noise intensity and correlation time on incipient separation behavior.   

 

Introduction 

1)The phenomenon of boundary-layer separation remains a central problem in modern aerodynamics (Schlichting & Gersten, 2016). Its occurrence is primarily associated with a severe degradation in aerodynamic performance, often characterized by a rapid increase in form drag and a substantial loss of lift (Anderson, 2016). In practical engineering, unexpected separation can lead to stall in aircraft wings or reduced efficiency in turbomachinery blades. Therefore, accurately predicting the onset of separation—especially under the influence of unpredictable environmental disturbances—is crucial for aerodynamic analysis and design. 

Physically, the onset of separation is governed by the inability of the low-momentum boundary-layer fluid to overcome an adverse pressure gradient (dp/dx > 0). In laminar similarity theory, this interaction is classically represented by the Falkner–Skan equation, where the pressure-gradient effect is parameterized by the constant β. A negative value of β represents a decelerating external flow, and as β approaches the critical threshold (β≈−0.1988), the wall shear stress vanishes, signaling the onset of separation.     

 

1） The phenomenon of boundary-layer separation remains a central problem in modern aerodynamics (Schlichting & Gersten, 2016). Its occurrence is primarily associated with a severe degradation in aerodynamic performance, often characterized by a rapid increase in form drag and a substantial loss of lift (Anderson, 2016). Physically, separation occurs when the low-momentum fluid within the boundary layer can no longer withstand an adverse pressure gradient. Since the onset of separation is closely linked to pressure-gradient effects, simplified theoretical models that isolate this mechanism are of particular interest. Within the framework of laminar similarity theory, one classical description is provided by the Falkner–Skan equation, in which the pressure-gradient effect is parameterized by the constant β. A negative value of β corresponds to a decelerating external flow, and as β approaches the critical threshold (β ≈ −0.1988), the wall shear stress vanishes, indicating the onset of separation. 

 

 

2） To isolate the role of the pressure gradient in a mathematically tractable manner, classical similarity models remain particularly useful. The Falkner-Skan model is adopted as the deterministic framework due to its unique ability to encapsulate the essential physics of boundary-layer separation through the variation of a single parameter,β. This mathematical conciseness allows for a rigorous examination of how stochastic uncertainties in the pressure environment propagate through the nonlinear boundary-layer system. A key advantage of this formulation is that the pressure-gradient influence is encoded in a single parameter, β, which enables a concise characterization of different external-flow regimes. In the decelerating-flow regime, β becomes negative, and the approach to the critical value β≈−0.1988 is associated with the disappearance of wall shear stress, marking incipient separation. While the model does not capture the full complexity of realistic separated flows, it remains especially valuable for mechanism-oriented analysis because it isolates the pressure-gradient effect in a mathematically tractable form. 

 

2） To study the influence of pressure-gradient effects in a reduced yet physically meaningful setting, classical similarity models remain particularly valuable. In this context, the Falkner–Skan model provides a canonical deterministic framework for steady laminar boundary layers developing under prescribed pressure-gradient conditions. Its main advantage lies in the fact that the external pressure-gradient influence is represented by a single parameter, β, which enables a concise description of different external-flow regimes within a unified formulation. In the decelerating-flow regime, β takes negative values, and as it approaches the critical value β≈−0.1988, the wall shear stress approaches zero, indicating incipient separation. Although the Falkner–Skan framework is inherently idealized and cannot reproduce the full complexity of realistic separated flows, it remains especially useful for mechanism-oriented analysis because it isolates the pressure-gradient effect in a transparent and mathematically tractable form. For this reason, it provides an appropriate deterministic baseline for the stochastic extension considered in the present study.
